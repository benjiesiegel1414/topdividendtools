Snowball Analytics vs Sharesight 2026: Which Dividend Tracker Is Better?

By Benjamin Siegel • Published October 22, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

If your main goal is tracking and forecasting dividend income, Snowball Analytics is the better pick. If you care most about accurate total returns, DRIP records and tax reports, Sharesight wins. Both have a free plan capped at 10 holdings, and both start around $7 a month when billed annually.

These two come up in almost every conversation about the best portfolio tracker for dividend investors, and they're the top two on my list of the best free dividend trackers. But they're built for different people. I've been a dividend investor for over ten years, and here's how I'd actually choose between them, with the real 2026 pricing, the limits nobody mentions, and who each one is for.

The Quick Verdict

  • Pick Snowball Analytics if you want to see your dividend income front and center: a payout calendar, future income projections, a dividend rating on each holding, and alerts when something is about to pay.
  • Pick Sharesight if you want your true return including dividends, reinvested DRIP shares and currency moves, plus tax reports you can hand to an accountant.
  • On a tight budget: both free plans are usable if you own 10 positions or fewer. Snowball's free plan is the friendlier of the two for a pure dividend portfolio.

The part most people miss: Sharesight's holding limits count closed positions too. Sell a stock and it still uses one of your 10 free slots (or 30 on Starter). If you trade around at all, you'll outgrow the cheaper Sharesight plans faster than you think. Snowball's paid plans have no holdings cap at all.

On price alone, Snowball is cheaper for most dividend investors. $79.99 a year gets you unlimited holdings, while Sharesight needs the $18-a-month Standard plan (about $216 a year) before holdings become unlimited.

Dividend Tracking Features

Snowball Analytics: built around the dividend

Snowball feels like it was designed by dividend investors. Even on the free plan you get automatic dividend tracking, a dividend calendar, received payouts, yield and yield on cost, and custom withholding tax. Paid plans add a dividend rating for each holding and push notifications for key dividend events, and the Investor plan adds email alerts too.

It also does the fun stuff: income projections, backtests on historical data, ETF look-through so you can see what's inside your funds, and a rebalancing tool. Paid plans let you link your brokerage so trades and dividends flow in automatically. On the free plan you import broker reports instead.

Sharesight: built around the real return

Sharesight tracks dividends and distributions automatically from 700,000 global stocks, ETFs and funds across 60+ exchanges, and estimates future dividends up to three years ahead in its future income report. There's a dividend calendar view as well.

Where it really shines is accuracy. It shows your true return including dividends, it tracks DRIP shares so you can see the compounding effect of reinvesting, and it calculates currency impacts on foreign holdings. It integrates with hundreds of brokers and apps, including Robinhood, Charles Schwab and Interactive Brokers, and you can also import trades by forwarding confirmation emails.

Taxes: Sharesight's Big Edge

This is the clearest difference between the two. Sharesight's paid plans all include tax reports, like taxable income and sold securities, and you can set the tax year to match where you live. The Standard and Premium plans also connect to Xero. The free plan has no tax reports.

Snowball handles withholding tax on dividends, which matters if you own foreign stocks, but it isn't built as a tax reporting tool. If tax time is a headache for you, especially with holdings in more than one country, that alone can justify Sharesight.

Who Each One Is For

Snowball Analytics is for the income builder. You own a mix of dividend stocks and ETFs, you reinvest, and the number you check most is "how much will I get paid this year?" That was me for most of the years I spent building toward living off dividends. Seeing every upcoming payout on a calendar keeps you motivated and keeps you from tinkering.

Sharesight is for the investor who wants the full truth. You hold across several brokers, maybe in more than one country, you use DRIPs, and you want to know your real annualized return after dividends, currency and fees. Or you just want tax season to take an hour instead of a weekend.

One thing I'd add: neither tool tells you whether an income ETF is actually worth owning. A tracker will happily show a fund paying you 15% while its share price slides every month. Before you add a high-yield fund, check its total return and price decay. That's why I built TopDividendETFsPRO, and you can get a free version of that view on TopDividendETFs.com.

My Pick

For most people reading this site, I'd start with Snowball Analytics. It's cheaper once you go past 10 holdings, it keeps dividends at the center, and the free plan is a real way to try it. Use the 14-day trial to link your broker and see if you like it.

I'd pick Sharesight instead if taxes, foreign holdings or exact performance numbers matter more to you than the dividend dashboard. It's the more serious accounting tool.

FAQ: Snowball Analytics vs Sharesight

Which investment portfolio trackers offer the best dividend tracking features?

For most dividend investors, Snowball Analytics and Sharesight are the two strongest. Snowball is built around dividends with a dividend calendar, income projections and a dividend rating. Sharesight is stronger on true total return, DRIP tracking, currency impacts and tax reports.

Is Snowball Analytics free?

Yes. Snowball Analytics has a free plan with 1 portfolio and up to 10 holdings, including the dividend calendar and broker report imports. Paid plans start at $79.99 a year ($7.99 billed monthly) and add unlimited holdings and live brokerage linking. Every paid plan has a 14-day free trial with no credit card.

Is Sharesight free?

Yes. Sharesight has a free plan with 1 portfolio and 10 holdings, and sold positions count toward that limit. Paid plans start at $7 a month billed annually ($9.33 billed monthly) and add tax reports, which the free plan does not include.

Is Snowball Analytics or Sharesight better for dividends?

If your main goal is watching and forecasting dividend income, I would pick Snowball Analytics. If you care most about accurate total return, DRIP records and tax time, especially with foreign holdings, Sharesight is the better fit.

What is the best dividend tracking website?

Snowball Analytics and Sharesight are the two I would start with. Both run in the browser and on mobile, both have free plans, and both track dividends automatically. For a wider list, see our ranking of the best free dividend trackers.
