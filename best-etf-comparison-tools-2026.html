Best ETF Comparison Tools 2026: How to Compare Dividend ETFs Side by Side

By Benjamin Siegel • Published November 2, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

The best free tools to compare dividend ETFs in 2026 are StockAnalysis's ETF compare tool for a quick side-by-side of yield, fees and returns, totalrealreturns.com for total return charts with dividends reinvested, and Portfolio Visualizer or testfol.io if you want to backtest whole portfolios. If you're comparing high-income ETFs, add a tool that flags price decay, because that's the number the others don't show you.

I compare dividend ETFs almost every day. And the one mistake I see over and over is people comparing yields. Yield is the least useful number on the screen. What you want to compare is total return: the dividends plus what happened to the share price. Every tool below is judged on how well it helps you do that, with real 2026 pricing from each company's own site.

What a Good Dividend ETF Comparison Tool Should Show

Before the list, here's what I actually look at when I put two dividend ETFs side by side:

  • Total return with dividends reinvested. This is the big one. A price-only chart makes every high-yield fund look terrible and every low-yield fund look great. Neither is the full picture.
  • Price decay. Is the share price slowly bleeding while the fund pays you a big yield? If so, part of that "income" is your own money coming back.
  • Expense ratio. The fee comes out every year whether the fund does well or not.
  • Yield and payout frequency. Useful, but only after the three above check out.
  • Holdings overlap. Two ETFs with different names can own a lot of the same stocks.

Here's a quick example of why yield alone misleads. In the TopDividendETFsPRO database, $SCHD yields about 3.2% and $VIG about 1.5%. If you only compared yield, you'd think $SCHD is twice as good. But both have averaged double-digit total returns per year since launch. They're built for different jobs, and only a total return comparison shows you that.

1. StockAnalysis ETF Compare: The Best Free Side-by-Side

If you just want to put $SCHD next to $VYM and see the basics, start here. The StockAnalysis compare tool lines up price, fund size, expense ratio, P/E and dividend yield, then shows average returns for year to date, 1, 5 and 10 years and since inception. It also lists the top 25 holdings of each fund, which is the easiest way I know to spot overlap.

The best part: StockAnalysis says its returns assume dividends are reinvested, so you're comparing total return, not just price. There are tabs for performance, price, dividends and technicals, plus a total return chart.

The compare page itself is free. Pro is $9.99 a month or $79 a year and adds things like longer dividend history and downloads, but you don't need it to compare funds. I also use StockAnalysis for dividend history in my roundup of the best dividend websites.

Use it for: a fast, free first look at two or three ETFs.

2. totalrealreturns.com: The Cleanest Total Return Chart

This one is simple and I love it for that. Type in a few tickers separated by commas and you get a chart of their historical returns with dividends reinvested and adjusted for inflation. You can switch either one off if you want to see the raw numbers.

It's free, and it does one job: show you what an investment actually grew to in real dollars. That's exactly the view that settles most "which dividend ETF is better" arguments. It doesn't show holdings, fees or yields, so pair it with StockAnalysis.

Use it for: ending the yield debate with a total return chart.

3. Portfolio Visualizer: Backtest Whole Portfolios

Comparing two funds is one thing. Comparing two portfolios, like 60% $SCHD and 40% $JEPQ versus 100% $VOO, is another. That's what Portfolio Visualizer is for. Its backtest compares up to 3 portfolios against a benchmark, with a toggle to reinvest dividends or take them as income, and options for rebalancing and regular withdrawals.

The free version needs no login. It's capped at 15 assets and limited history. Paid plans start at $30 a month billed annually for personal use, which is a lot for most individual investors. The free version covers what most of us need.

Use it for: testing what a full income portfolio would have done, including withdrawals.

4. testfol.io: Free Backtests With Full History

testfol.io is the newer backtester a lot of investors have switched to. The free plan needs no card and includes the full available history and the main backtest tools, plus a limited Monte Carlo simulation. What you give up is saving runs and downloads.

Pro is $12.50 a month billed annually, and the site says that's early-member pricing that's expected to go up. If you backtest a lot and want to save your work, it's cheaper than Portfolio Visualizer's paid plans.

Use it for: long-history backtests on a budget.

5. Koyfin: For People Who Want a Terminal

Koyfin is more of a full market data platform than a comparison tool. The free plan includes advanced charting, portfolios, 2 watchlists and 2 screens. ETF holdings and an ETF screener come with Plus at $39 a month, and Koyfin says annual plans save up to 30%.

For comparing a handful of dividend ETFs, that's overkill. But if you already like charts and dashboards, the free plan is worth a look.

How to Compare Two Dividend ETFs in 5 Steps

  1. Pick the same time period. A fund that launched in 2022 can't be fairly compared on a 10-year chart. Use the longest period both funds have existed.
  2. Compare total return, dividends reinvested. totalrealreturns.com or the StockAnalysis total return chart.
  3. Check price decay. If the share price keeps falling while the yield stays high, be careful.
  4. Check fees and overlap. Owning two funds with the same top 10 isn't diversification.
  5. Then look at yield and frequency. Only now does it matter whether it pays 3% quarterly or 12% monthly.

Once you've picked, model what it could actually pay you. DividendProjection.com shows what a given amount in a dividend ETF could pay over time, and the best DRIP calculators show what reinvesting does over 10 or 20 years. If you like getting paid often, MonthlyETFs.com and WeeklyETFs.com list the monthly and weekly payers.

FAQ: Dividend ETF Comparison Tools

Is there a free tool to compare ETFs side by side?

Yes. StockAnalysis's ETF compare tool is free and lines up yield, expense ratio, returns and top holdings. totalrealreturns.com is free for total return charts with dividends reinvested, and Portfolio Visualizer and testfol.io have free backtesting plans.

What is the best dividend comparison tool?

For a quick free side-by-side, StockAnalysis. For total return charts, totalrealreturns.com. For high-income ETFs, use a tool that flags price decay, like TopDividendETFs.com or TopDividendETFsPRO, because yield alone can hide a falling share price.

How do I compare two dividend ETFs?

Use the same time period, compare total return with dividends reinvested, check for price decay, then compare fees and holdings overlap. Look at yield and payout frequency last.

Does Portfolio Visualizer include dividends?

Yes. Its backtest has a setting to reinvest dividends or take them as income, so you can compare total return or see the income a portfolio would have paid.

Why do ETF comparison sites show different returns?

Mostly because of time periods and whether dividends are reinvested. A price-only chart leaves out the dividends, which makes high-yield funds look much worse than they really did.
