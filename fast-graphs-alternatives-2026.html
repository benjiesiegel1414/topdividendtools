FAST Graphs Alternatives 2026: 5 Cheaper Ways to Value Dividend Stocks

The best FAST Graphs alternatives in 2026 are Stock Unlock if you want valuation scores, a DCF calculator and dividend history for less money, Simply Wall St if you want visual valuation reports with a free plan, Seeking Alpha Premium if you want valuation grades plus broad research, StockAnalysis if you want clean financial data and decades of dividend history for $79 a year, and a free stack if you only check valuation a few times a year. FAST Graphs itself starts at $15.95 a month for Basic and $40 a month for Premium, both billed annually.

The idea behind it is one of the best ideas in dividend investing: line up the stock price against earnings and cash flow, and you can see in one picture whether you're overpaying. What FAST Graphs Actually Gives You (and What It Costs)

FAST Graphs stands for Fundamental Analysis Software Tool. Its signature chart plots a company's share price against its earnings (or cash flow) history, with a "normal" valuation line. When the price sits well above the earnings line, the stock looks expensive. When it dips below, it looks cheap compared to its own history. Simple, and very effective for dividend growth stocks.

Here's what its pricing page listed when I checked on November 17, 2026:

  • Free trial: 7 days, no credit card required, with the full feature set.
  • Basic: starting at $15.95 a month, billed annually. 3 basic portfolios, 5 saved screens, no broker connections, and no analyst sentiment or EBITDA and sales data.
  • Premium: starting at $40 a month, billed annually (marked "Most Popular"). 5 broker connections, 50 advanced portfolios, unlimited screens, analyst sentiment and estimate revisions, EBITDA, EBIT and sales metrics, and compare-to-company charts.

There's no free plan after the trial. The annual toggle says you save 20% versus paying monthly. And this is the part most people miss: the stuff most dividend investors want, like broker connections and the extra valuation metrics, sits in Premium. So the real comparison is usually about $480 a year, not $191.

1. Stock Unlock: The Closest Swap on a Budget

If what you love about FAST Graphs is "is this stock cheap right now?", Stock Unlock gets you closest for the least money. It scores stocks across categories including valuation and dividends, has a DCF calculator that can set buy targets and alert you when a stock hits one, and lets you chart any metric over time with its free form graphing.

Its pricing page lists a free plan (limited financial and dividend data, 3 screener searches a week, a limited DCF calculator), Pro at $7.50 a month (10 years of financial and dividend data, 1 linked broker, unlimited DCF and screener) and Unlimited at $20 a month (35 years of financial and dividend data, unlimited linked brokers). Annual billing saves 17%, and every paid plan has a 30 day money-back guarantee.

What you give up: the FAST Graphs chart itself. That earnings line overlaid on price is still the cleanest way I've seen to judge a dividend growth stock in five seconds. Stock Unlock makes you piece it together from scores, DCF and graphs. But at Pro pricing, you'd pay a fraction of FAST Graphs Premium. I also included it in my Simply Safe Dividends alternatives list for the same reason: a lot of tool for the money.

Pick it if: you want valuation, dividend data and portfolio tracking in one place for under $100 a year.

2. Simply Wall St: Visual Valuation With a Real Free Plan

Simply Wall St turns a company into a visual report: its "snowflake" graphic, a fair value estimate, a dividend section with payout coverage and history, and a portfolio view. If you think in pictures like FAST Graphs users tend to, this one feels familiar.

Its plans page shows three tiers. The Free plan gives you 1 portfolio with 10 holdings, 5 company or ETF reports a month and the top 4 results per screener. Premium raises that to 3 portfolios, 30 holdings, 30 reports a month and unlimited dividend analysis. Unlimited removes the caps. There's a 7 day Premium trial with no card, and on day 8 you drop back to the free plan automatically. Prices vary by region and billing, so check the plans page for yours.

The catch: its fair value leans on a DCF model, and DCFs are only as good as the assumptions going in. I'd use it as a second opinion, not as the answer.

Pick it if: you own a handful of stocks and want a visual valuation check that costs nothing to start.

3. Seeking Alpha Premium: Valuation Plus Everything Else

Seeking Alpha Premium doesn't draw the FAST Graphs chart, but its Quant Ratings include a Valuation grade, and its Dividend Grades score safety, growth, yield and consistency. Add the screener, earnings call transcripts and alerts, and you've got a full research desk for about $299 a year at list price, with discounts common.

The tradeoff is noise. Thousands of contributors means great analysis next to people talking their own book. You have to filter. I reviewed it in depth for income investors over on TopDividendETFs.com: Seeking Alpha Premium review for dividend investors.

Pick it if: valuation is one of several things you research and you want it all under one login.

4. StockAnalysis: The Raw Data, Cheap

Here's what I actually look at when I judge valuation myself: P/E versus the stock's own history, earnings growth, payout ratio and how long the dividend has been growing. StockAnalysis gives you all of it in a clean layout. Financial statements, ratios, dividend history and screeners are free to browse.

Pro is $9.99 a month or $79 a year and unlocks 10 to 50 years of dividend history, brokerage holdings sync, bigger watchlists and data downloads (1 a day on Pro). An Unlimited tier removes the download and watchlist limits. There's no free trial, but there's a 60 day money-back guarantee.

You won't get a pretty valuation chart. You'll get the numbers behind one, which honestly is enough if you're willing to spend 10 minutes per stock. It's also one of my picks in the best dividend screeners.

Pick it if: you're comfortable reading a table and want decades of dividend data for $79 a year.

5. The Free Stack: A 10 Minute Valuation Check

Before paying anyone, try this on the next stock you're looking at:

  1. Compare today's P/E to its 5 or 10 year average. StockAnalysis and Yahoo Finance both show the history. Way above average? You're paying up.
  2. Check the dividend yield against its own history. For steady dividend growers, a yield well above its usual range often means the price is low (or the market smells trouble).
  3. Check the payout ratio and free cash flow. Cheap and unsafe is a trap, not a bargain.
  4. Look at earnings growth. A slow grower doesn't deserve a growth stock multiple.

That's 80% of what the FAST Graphs chart tells you, done by hand. For more free sites, see my ranking of the best dividend websites.

Pick it if: you buy a few stocks a year and don't mind a little homework.

FAQ: FAST Graphs Alternatives

What are the best FAST Graphs alternatives?

Stock Unlock (free plan, Pro listed at $7.50 a month) for valuation scores and a DCF calculator, Simply Wall St (free plan plus Premium) for visual valuation reports, Seeking Alpha Premium (about $299 a year list price) for valuation grades and broad research, StockAnalysis Pro ($79 a year) for financial data and long dividend history, and a free do-it-yourself P/E and payout ratio check.

How much does FAST Graphs cost?

As of November 17, 2026, FAST Graphs Basic starts at $15.95 a month and Premium at $40 a month, both billed annually, after a 7 day free trial with no credit card. Paying annually saves 20% versus monthly. There is no free plan after the trial.

Is FAST Graphs worth it for dividend investors?

It can be if you buy individual dividend growth stocks often and check valuation before every purchase. Its price versus earnings charts make overvaluation obvious. If you mostly own ETFs or buy a few stocks a year, a cheaper tool or a free valuation check is usually enough.

Is there a free alternative to FAST Graphs?

Yes. Stock Unlock and Simply Wall St both have free plans, and StockAnalysis and Yahoo Finance show P/E history, payout ratios and dividend history for free. Comparing today's P/E and yield to their own 5 to 10 year averages covers most of what the FAST Graphs chart shows.

What is the difference between FAST Graphs Basic and Premium?

Premium adds 5 broker connections, 50 advanced portfolios, unlimited saved screens, analyst sentiment and estimate revisions, and EBITDA, EBIT and sales metrics. Basic has 3 basic portfolios, 5 saved screens and no broker connections.
