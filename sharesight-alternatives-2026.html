Sharesight Alternatives 2026: 6 Dividend Trackers Worth Switching To

By Benjie • Published November 10, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

The best Sharesight alternatives in 2026 are Snowball Analytics if you want a dividend-first dashboard with unlimited holdings for $79.99 a year, Stock Events for the cheapest full app at $49.99 a year, and Portseido if you need Sharesight-style global performance tracking for less. For income-focused investors, MerryDiv, HeyDividend and Income Calendar each do one dividend job better than Sharesight does.

Sharesight is a good tracker. I'm not here to trash it. It nails true returns, DRIP tracking, currencies and tax reports. But it's built for performance and tax reporting first, and dividends second. And the moment you go past 10 holdings, including positions you already sold, you're paying. So here's what I'd look at instead, depending on why you're leaving, with pricing I pulled from each company's own site.

This is the part most people miss. Sharesight counts closed holdings toward your limit. If you've been investing for a few years and trimmed or sold a handful of positions, a "10 holding" or "30 holding" plan fills up fast. A lot of dividend investors end up on Standard, which is $216 a year. That's usually the moment people start searching for alternatives.

Why People Look for a Sharesight Alternative

In my experience it's one of these four:

  • Price. Unlimited holdings means the $18 a month Standard plan.
  • It's not dividend-first. Sharesight does have a future income report and a dividend calendar. But the product is centered on performance and tax, not on "how much will I get paid next month."
  • Income ETF blind spots. If you own high yield or option income ETFs, you want to see payouts next to NAV erosion. That's not Sharesight's focus.
  • You just want a phone app. Some people want to open their phone and see the income number. That's it.

Pick your reason first. It'll pick the tool for you.

1. Snowball Analytics: The Best All-Around Alternative for Dividend Investors

If you want a tracker that's built around dividends from the ground up, this is where I'd start. Snowball pulls your holdings into one dashboard with a dividend calendar, received payouts, yield on cost, custom withholding tax and future income projections. It can link to 1,000+ brokerages on paid plans, or import broker reports on the free plan.

The pricing is the big difference. Snowball's Starter plan is $79.99 a year and gives you unlimited holdings in one portfolio. To get unlimited holdings on Sharesight you need Standard at $18 a month billed annually, which is $216 a year. If you need multiple portfolios, Snowball's Investor plan is $149.99 a year for 10.

Where Sharesight still wins: tax reports and the depth of its performance math. I broke down that exact matchup in Snowball Analytics vs Sharesight.

Pick it if: dividends are the main reason you track, and you'd rather pay $80 a year than $216.

2. Stock Events: The Cheapest Full-Featured Option

Stock Events is the value pick. The free plan tracks up to 15 stocks, five more than Sharesight's free plan, with watchlist, portfolio, dividend and event tracking. Pro is $49.99 a year and unlocks unlimited stocks, portfolio analytics and its advanced dividend features.

The catch: automatic brokerage syncing is a separate add-on called Connect at $0.99 a week. Without it you update holdings by hand. If you buy a few times a month, that's no big deal. Users love its dividend calendar screen, and it shows.

Pick it if: you want a clean app and a great dividend calendar for as little money as possible.

3. Portseido: The Closest Like-for-Like Replacement

If what you like about Sharesight is the performance math and global coverage, Portseido is the most similar tool on this list. It covers stocks on 70+ exchanges and 20,000+ ETFs, records dividends and splits automatically, and lets you measure simple, time-weighted or money-weighted returns against a benchmark.

Its free plan works differently: it caps transactions (100 total) instead of holdings, and it includes broker syncing and full reports. Paid plans are $8 a month (Plus, unlimited transactions, one portfolio) or $12 a month (Pro, multiple portfolios), billed annually, with a 14-day free trial and no card.

Pick it if: you hold international stocks or ETFs, care about accurate returns, and don't need Sharesight's tax reports.

4. MerryDiv: Best for Monthly Income Across Several Accounts

MerryDiv is built for one question: how much passive income am I making each month? It links to brokers through Plaid with read-only access and rolls everything into a monthly income dashboard with a forward 12 month projection. That's handy if your dividends are spread across a Roth, a 401(k) and a taxable account. It also has a dividend calendar and research lists like Dividend Aristocrats and Kings.

The free plan syncs one brokerage. Paid plans start at $5.99 a month, or $59.99 a year.

Pick it if: you think about your portfolio as a paycheck, not a performance chart.

5. HeyDividend: Best for High Yield and Income ETF Investors

This is the one I'd look at if your portfolio is full of high yield ETFs. HeyDividend covers stocks, ETFs, CEFs, BDCs and REITs, and pairs forward income projections with NAV erosion analysis, dividend scores and an AI chat assistant. That NAV erosion piece is exactly what Sharesight doesn't put front and center.

The free plan covers one portfolio with up to 25 holdings, which is more room than Sharesight's free plan. Pro is $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year and adds unlimited portfolios, real-time data and Plaid broker sync. New accounts get a 14-day Pro trial.

Pick it if: you own covered call or option income funds and want to watch the share price as closely as the payout.

6. Income Calendar: Best for Retirees Planning Cash Flow

Income Calendar, from BNK Invest, flips the usual tracker around. Instead of showing what you were paid, it projects every dividend your portfolio is expected to pay over the next 12 months, by month, by position and down to the penny, with projected ex-dividend and pay dates on a calendar. You can see the light months coming before they hit your bank account.

There's a free preview for up to 10 holdings, and the full version is $8.99 a month with unlimited holdings and accounts. I covered it alongside other forecasting tools in the best dividend income calculators guide.

Pick it if: you're living on dividends, or close to it, and budgeting month to month.

Free and unlimited: a spreadsheet

If you'd rather pay nothing and control everything, a Google Sheets tracker still works. No holding limits, no subscription. The cost is your time. I walked through the spreadsheet vs app decision in how to track dividends in 2026.

My honest take on switching: the best tracker is the one you'll actually keep updated. Don't move just to save $5 a month if Sharesight's tax reports save you an afternoon every April. But if you're paying $216 a year mostly to see your dividend income, a dividend-first tool will probably serve you better for less.

FAQ: Sharesight Alternatives

What is the best alternative to Sharesight?

For dividend investors, Snowball Analytics is the best all-around alternative: unlimited holdings on its $79.99 a year Starter plan versus $216 a year for Sharesight's Standard plan. Stock Events is the cheapest full app at $49.99 a year, and Portseido is the closest match for global performance tracking.

Is there a free alternative to Sharesight?

Yes. Stock Events tracks up to 15 stocks free, HeyDividend's free plan covers 25 holdings, Portseido's free plan allows 100 transactions with broker sync, and a Google Sheets tracker has no limits at all. Sharesight's own free plan caps you at 10 holdings, including closed positions.

Which investment portfolio trackers offer the best dividend tracking features?

Snowball Analytics, MerryDiv, HeyDividend and Income Calendar are built around dividends: payout calendars, forward income projections and monthly income views. Sharesight and Portseido are stronger on performance math and global coverage.

How much does Sharesight cost?

As of November 2026, Sharesight is free for 1 portfolio and 10 holdings. Starter is $7 a month billed annually for 30 holdings, Standard is $18 a month for 4 portfolios and unlimited holdings, and Premium is $23.25 a month for 10 portfolios. Closed holdings count toward the limits.

Is Snowball Analytics better than Sharesight?

For a dividend-focused dashboard with income projections at a lower price, usually yes. For tax reports, DRIP accounting and detailed performance across currencies, Sharesight is stronger.
