The problem we set out to solve

If you invest for dividends, you already know the routine. You want to check an ex-dividend date, so you open one site. You want to model what a position throws off over ten years with reinvestment, so you open another. You want to see whether a payout is actually covered by earnings, so you open a third. You want to track the whole portfolio in one place, so you open a spreadsheet from 2019 that never quite got finished.

None of those tools talk to each other. Half of them are abandoned. A few are excellent and almost nobody has heard of them, because they're built by small developers with no marketing budget. And when you search for "best dividend tracker," you mostly get affiliate pages written by people who have clearly never used a single product on the list.

That's the gap. Not a shortage of tools — a shortage of anyone honestly organizing them.

What this site is: a curated, continuously updated directory of dividend investing tools — trackers, calculators, screeners, calendars, and research platforms — with plain descriptions of what each one actually does, what it costs, and who it's for.

What this site is not: a brokerage, an advisory service, or a place that will tell you what to buy.

How tools get listed and ranked

We want to be direct about this, because most directories are vague on purpose.

  • Anyone can be listed for free. Tools are added because we found them useful, because a reader recommended one, or because a developer submitted it through the Submit Tool form. Listing costs nothing.
  • Sponsored placements are labeled. A small number of slots at the top of the directory are paid. Every one of them carries a visible SPONSORED badge. If it isn't labeled, nobody paid for it.
  • Rankings are editorial, and therefore subjective. We weigh how useful a tool is to an ordinary dividend investor, how current its data is, how much it costs relative to what it delivers, and whether it's actively maintained. Reasonable people will disagree with some of our calls. That's fine — the descriptions are there so you can decide for yourself.
  • Corrections get made. Pricing changes, tools get shut down, features get added. If something on this site is wrong, tell us and we'll fix it.

How the site makes money

Running this costs money and time, so here's exactly how it's funded — no mystery:

  • Sponsored listings and banner placements from ETF issuers and tool developers, always labeled as such. If you're interested, see the advertise page.
  • Display advertising served through third-party ad networks.
  • Affiliate links. Some outbound links to tools pay a commission if you sign up. It never costs you extra, and it never buys a higher ranking.

Sponsorship buys visibility. It does not buy a favorable review, and it does not remove the badge that tells you it was paid for.

Our one promise to you

We will never tell you what to buy. We're not financial advisors, we're not registered as one, and anyone on the internet who confidently tells a stranger what to do with their retirement account should be treated with suspicion.

What we will do is put accurate, current, well-organized information in front of you, label the paid stuff honestly, and let you make your own decisions. That's the whole business model.
