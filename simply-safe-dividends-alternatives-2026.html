1. Snowball Analytics: The Cheapest Paid Option With a Dividend Rating

If what you really want is a portfolio tracker that also tells you which holdings look shaky, Snowball is where I'd start. It's still my default tracker pick for most dividend investors, and it's number one on my list of the best free dividend trackers.

The free plan gives you 1 portfolio and 10 holdings, but the dividend rating is a paid feature. Paid plans are $79.99 a year for Starter, $149.99 for Investor and $249.99 for Expert, all with a 14-day free trial and no card required. Even the top tier is about half the price of Simply Safe Dividends.

Be clear on what you're getting, though. Snowball's dividend rating is a quick read, not a full research write-up. You also get a dividend calendar, income projections, DRIP modeling and broker syncing on paid plans. If you're also weighing Snowball against other trackers, I covered that in Snowball Analytics alternatives and Snowball Analytics vs Sharesight.

Pick it if: you want one app to track your income and flag obvious risks, for under $100 a year.

2. Seeking Alpha Premium: Dividend Grades Plus Everything Else

Seeking Alpha Premium is the closest thing to a full replacement. Its Dividend Grades score every stock on safety, growth, yield and consistency, and you also get Quant Ratings, the stock screener with dividend filters, earnings call transcripts, alerts and portfolio linking. The list price is about $299 a year, and first-year discounts are common.

Here's the tradeoff. Simply Safe Dividends is built by a small team focused only on dividends. Seeking Alpha is a giant platform with thousands of authors, so you get way more coverage and way more noise. Some articles are excellent. Some are written by people talking their own book. You have to sort through it.

My sister site has a full Seeking Alpha Premium review for dividend investors if you want the deeper breakdown.

Pick it if: you research individual stocks a lot and want dividend safety as one part of a bigger toolkit.

3. FAST Graphs: For Investors Who Worry About Overpaying

FAST Graphs doesn't give you a single safety score. What it gives you is the picture behind one: years of earnings, cash flow and dividends plotted against the share price. If earnings have been falling while the dividend keeps rising, you can see the squeeze coming with your own eyes. That's how most dividend cuts start.

Pricing on its site starts at $15.95 a month billed annually for Basic and $40 a month billed annually for Premium, with a 7-day free trial and no card needed. One catch: the dividend research and Income tab are on the free trial and Premium, not Basic. So for a dividend investor, Premium is really the plan to compare, and at about $480 a year it isn't cheaper. You'd choose it for the valuation charts, not to save money.

Pick it if: you're a dividend growth investor who cares as much about the price you pay as the dividend you get.

4. Stock Unlock: The Budget All-in-One

Stock Unlock covers more than 170,000 stocks and ETFs across 70+ exchanges and scores each one across seven categories, with dividends as one of them. You also get a portfolio tracker that forecasts upcoming dividend payments, a screener and an automated DCF calculator.

There's a free plan with watchlists, portfolios and limited dividend history, plus paid Pro and Unlimited tiers that add longer history, unlimited Stock Scores and brokerage connections. Stock Unlock shows prices in your local currency, so check its pricing page for your number. Paid plans come with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

It won't match Simply Safe Dividends on dividend-specific depth. There's no dedicated cut-risk score. But for a newer investor who wants fundamentals, dividend data and tracking in one cheap place, it's a solid deal.

Pick it if: you want decent research and tracking without paying for a premium service.

5. The Free DIY Dividend Safety Check

This is the part most people miss: you can do a pretty good dividend safety check yourself in about ten minutes per stock, for free. Here's what I actually look at:

  • Payout ratio. What share of earnings goes to the dividend. For most companies, a ratio that keeps climbing toward or over 100% is a warning sign. REITs and utilities run higher, so compare within the industry.
  • Free cash flow. Dividends are paid in cash, not accounting earnings. If free cash flow doesn't cover the dividend for a couple of years in a row, be careful.
  • Debt. Rising debt plus a rising dividend can mean they're borrowing to pay you.
  • Dividend history. Did they cut in 2008 or 2020? How a company behaved in the last two recessions tells you a lot.
  • The yield itself. A yield way above the company's own history usually means the price fell for a reason. The market is often telling you something.

Free dividend websites like Yahoo Finance and StockAnalysis show most of these numbers, and one of the best dividend screeners can help you filter by payout ratio before you dig in. Log what you find in your tracker of choice; my guide to the best dividend portfolio tracker covers which ones handle this well.

Pick it if: you own 5 to 20 stocks and don't mind doing a little homework.

FAQ: Simply Safe Dividends Alternatives

What is a cheaper alternative to Simply Safe Dividends?

Snowball Analytics is the cheapest paid option with a dividend rating, starting at $79.99 a year versus $468 a year for Simply Safe Dividends. Seeking Alpha Premium, at about $299 a year, adds Dividend Grades plus full stock research. For $0, you can check payout ratios and dividend history yourself with free tools.

Is Simply Safe Dividends worth $468 a year?

It can be if you own a lot of individual dividend stocks, because avoiding one big dividend cut can be worth more than the subscription. If you mostly own dividend ETFs, you are paying for stock-level safety research you will rarely use.

Does Simply Safe Dividends have a free trial?

Yes. Simply Safe Dividends offers a free trial with no credit card required, and first-time subscribers get a 60-day money-back guarantee. The paid plan is annual only at $468 a year, which works out to $39 a month.

Which dividend tracker has a dividend safety score?

Simply Safe Dividends is best known for its Dividend Safety Scores. Snowball Analytics includes a dividend rating on its paid plans, and Seeking Alpha Premium includes Dividend Grades covering safety, growth, yield and consistency.
