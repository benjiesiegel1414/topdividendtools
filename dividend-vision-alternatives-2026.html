Dividend Vision Alternatives 2026: 6 Dividend Trackers Worth a Look

By Benjie • Published November 19, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

The best Dividend Vision alternatives in 2026 are Snowball Analytics if you want a dividend dashboard with real phone apps, Hey Dividend if you hold a lot of high-yield ETFs and CEFs, DivTracker for the cheapest paid plan, Dividend Watch for a simple annual price, and Sharesight if you need tax reports. If you mainly use Dividend Vision to screen income ETFs, a dedicated ETF tool may serve you better than another tracker.

Dividend Vision is a solid tool, especially for ETF-heavy income investors. But it's web only, the free plan keeps your portfolio in your browser with no broker connection, and the paid plans run $7.95 to $9.95 a month. So here's what I'd look at instead, depending on what's actually bugging you, with real 2026 pricing from each company's own site.

What Dividend Vision Does (and What It Costs)

Before switching, it helps to know exactly what you'd be giving up. Dividend Vision is a dividend income tracker plus research site. It tracks your holdings and income, forecasts your portfolio "paycheck" by month, quarter and year, and has screeners for ETFs, REITs and closed-end funds. Its pricing page says it covers 13,200+ assets, including 5,600+ ETFs from 700+ fund issuers, and fund pages show a Distribution Safety Score.

Broker connections on Plus and Pro are read-only through Plaid and SnapTrade, and the FAQ names Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, E*Trade, Robinhood, Interactive Brokers and Merrill Edge. There's no native iOS or Android app mentioned on the site. You use it in a browser, including on your phone.

Why People Look for a Dividend Vision Alternative

From what I see in the searches that land on this site, it usually comes down to one of these:

  • No mobile app. If you want to open an app and see your dividend income, a web-only tool feels clunky.
  • Free plan limits. The free portfolio lives in your browser. Switch devices or clear your browser and it's not synced. Cloud save and broker sync start at $7.95 a month.
  • Price. It's not expensive, but if all you need is income tracking, some apps do that for less.
  • Different focus. Some people want deep research on individual dividend stocks, or tax reports, which isn't what Dividend Vision is built for.

Figure out which of those is your real reason first. That one answer picks the tool for you.

1. Snowball Analytics: The Best All-Around Alternative

If you want the closest thing to Dividend Vision with real phone apps, start here. Snowball pulls your accounts into one dashboard, connects to 1,000+ brokerages through aggregators like Plaid, Yodlee and SnapTrade, and also takes CSV, Excel and PDF statement imports. For income investors it has a payout calendar, future income projections, a dividend safety rating and DRIP modeling. It also has ETF look-through, so you can see what's actually inside the funds you own.

The free plan is 1 portfolio and 10 holdings, without broker linking. Paid plans start at $79.99 a year with a 14-day free trial, which is almost the same price as Dividend Vision Plus on annual billing. It's still number one on my list of the best free dividend trackers. Already on Snowball and not happy? I covered Snowball Analytics alternatives too.

Pick it if: you want one dashboard on web and phone, synced with your brokers.

2. Hey Dividend: The Pick for High-Yield ETF Investors

Hey Dividend is the most similar in spirit to Dividend Vision. It covers stocks, ETFs, CEFs, BDCs and REITs, and it has something I care a lot about: NAV erosion analysis for income ETFs. It also has an AI chat assistant, machine learning dividend scores, forward income projections and a dividend calendar.

The free plan is more generous than most: 1 portfolio with up to 25 holdings. Pro is $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year and adds unlimited portfolios, real-time data and broker sync through Plaid. Every new account gets a 14-day Pro trial.

This is the part most people miss with high-yield funds: the yield number means nothing if the share price keeps sliding. Any tool that puts NAV erosion next to the yield is doing you a favor.

Pick it if: your portfolio is full of covered call ETFs, CEFs and BDCs and you want an app on your phone.

3. DivTracker: The Cheapest Paid Option

DivTracker is a dividend tracker for iOS, iPad and the web. The free plan is tight at 1 portfolio and 5 holdings. But the paid plans are the cheapest on this list: Pro is $29.99 billed annually for unlimited portfolios and holdings, and Ultimate is $49.99 billed annually and adds linking your US brokerage accounts. There's a 3-day free trial on Ultimate, and no one-time payment option.

It shows your dividend income on a calendar, sends iOS notifications on payment dates, syncs across devices and exports to CSV. It isn't as deep as Dividend Vision on ETF research, but for tracking income it does the job for about a third of the price.

Pick it if: you're on iPhone and just want to see your dividend income without paying much. Android users should look elsewhere, since the pricing page doesn't mention an Android app.

4. Dividend Watch: Simple Annual Pricing

Dividend Watch only sells annual plans. Free gets you 1 portfolio and 10 holdings. Premium is $72 a year for 3 portfolios and unlimited holdings, and Pro is $99 a year for unlimited portfolios and 15 years of financial statement history. Both paid plans have a 7-day trial. The pricing page lists dividend forecasts, a dividend calendar, broker sync, a safety score, DRIP support, custom tax rates and multi-currency among its features, and the FAQ says there's no lifetime plan right now.

It leans more toward individual dividend stocks than Dividend Vision does, with financial statement history and stock safety scores.

Pick it if: you own mostly dividend stocks and like paying once a year.

5. Sharesight: The Pick for Taxes and True Returns

Sharesight is a different kind of tool. It's built around accurate performance and tax reporting. It records dividends automatically across more than 700,000 stocks, ETFs and funds on 60+ exchanges, tracks reinvested dividends, handles multiple currencies, and imports from hundreds of brokers. The free plan covers 1 portfolio and 10 holdings, and paid plans start at $7 a month billed annually.

If you want to know your real return including dividends, not just your income, this is the one. I compared it head to head in Snowball Analytics vs Sharesight.

Pick it if: you hold across several brokers or countries, you DRIP, or tax time is a pain.

What No Tracker Will Tell You

I've been a dividend investor for over ten years, and the biggest lesson is this: a tracker only shows you what you already own. It'll happily show a fund paying you 15% while the share price slides every month and quietly eats your capital.

Before you add any high-yield ETF, check its total return and price decay. You can compare any two funds on total return at ETFTotalReturns.com. To see what a given amount in a dividend ETF could pay you over time, try DividendProjection.com. And if you like getting paid often, MonthlyETFs.com and WeeklyETFs.com track the monthly and weekly payers.

What are the best Dividend Vision alternatives?

How much does Dividend Vision cost?

Dividend Vision has a free plan, Plus at $7.95 a month or $76.32 a year, and Pro at $9.95 a month or $95.52 a year, per its pricing page as of November 19, 2026. New accounts get a 7-day Pro trial with no credit card.

Does Dividend Vision have a mobile app?

Yes. It covers 5,600+ ETFs from 700+ fund issuers, with ETF screeners and a Distribution Safety Score on fund pages.
