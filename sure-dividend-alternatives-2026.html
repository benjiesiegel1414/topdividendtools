Sure Dividend Alternatives 2026: 5 Cheaper Ways to Research Dividend Stocks

By Benjie • Published November 15, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

The best Sure Dividend alternatives in 2026 are Simply Safe Dividends if you want dividend safety scores for $468 a year, Seeking Alpha Premium if you want broad stock research with Dividend Grades for about $299 a year, FAST Graphs if valuation is your main concern, Snowball Analytics if you mostly want to track income (free plan, paid from $79.99 a year), and a free do-it-yourself stack if you own mostly ETFs. Every one of them costs less than Sure Dividend's flagship research database at $999 a year.

Sure Dividend is a serious shop. I'm not here to trash it. Its free dividend lists are some of the most useful pages on the internet for dividend investors, and the premium research is thorough. But $999 a year is a big number, and a lot of people searching for "Sure Dividend alternatives" really want one piece of what it does, not the whole thing. So let's figure out which piece you need and what it costs elsewhere.

What Sure Dividend Actually Gives You (and What It Costs)

Sure Dividend's top product is the Sure Analysis Research Database. Here's what its subscribe page lists:

  • A 13 person research team covering 900+ income securities, including every Dividend King, Champion and Aristocrat
  • A 3 page PDF report on each security, updated quarterly (3,600+ reports a year)
  • A daily updated spreadsheet with 5 year expected total returns, fair value estimates and Dividend Risk scores
  • All of its premium newsletters: Sure Dividend Core, High Yield and Growth
  • A weekly "Top 10" email of the highest expected return picks with A or B Dividend Risk scores

On the checkout page, the price was 7 days free, then $999 a year when I checked on November 15, 2026. The refund terms are fair: a full refund in the first 60 days, then prorated refunds after that. The company says it has more than 7,000 active premium members.

And then there's the free side. Sure Dividend publishes lists like the Dividend Champions, monthly dividend stocks, REITs, MLPs and BDCs, many marked as updated daily. Those cost nothing, and honestly, for a lot of investors, they're the most valuable part.

This is the part most people miss: you don't have to choose between "pay $999" and "get nothing." You can use the free lists for ideas, then use a cheaper tool for the job you actually care about.

1. Simply Safe Dividends: The Closest Swap for Dividend Safety

If the reason you want Sure Dividend is its Dividend Risk scores, Simply Safe Dividends is the most direct replacement, and it's less than half the price.

Its Dividend Safety Scores rate each company from 0 to 100 using payout ratios, debt, recession performance, dividend history, free cash flow and analyst estimates. The company publishes its track record and says companies scoring above 60 since 2015 avoided 97% of dividend cuts. You also get cut alerts, an income calendar and forecast, screeners and a newsletter with model portfolios.

Pricing is simple: $39 a month, billed annually at $468. No monthly plan. There's a 14 day free trial with no card and a 60 day money-back guarantee for first-time subscribers.

What you give up versus Sure Dividend: fewer "here's the expected 5 year total return" style rankings. Simply Safe Dividends is about not getting your income cut, not about ranking the best buys. I compared it head to head with a tracker in Simply Safe Dividends vs Snowball Analytics, and if it's still too pricey, see my list of Simply Safe Dividends alternatives.

Pick it if: you own individual U.S. dividend stocks and a cut is your biggest fear.

2. Seeking Alpha Premium: More Coverage for Less Money

Seeking Alpha Premium is the "everything" option. Its Dividend Grades score stocks on safety, growth, yield and consistency, and you also get Quant Ratings, a screener with dividend filters, earnings call transcripts and portfolio alerts. The list price is about $299 a year, and first-year discounts are common.

The tradeoff is focus. Sure Dividend is a dedicated team with one consistent framework across every report. Seeking Alpha has thousands of contributors, so you get far more coverage and far more noise. Some articles are excellent. Some are written by people talking their own book. You have to filter.

Pick it if: you research a lot of individual stocks and want dividend data as one part of a bigger toolkit.

3. FAST Graphs: For Investors Who Care About Price Paid

One thing I like about Sure Dividend's approach is that it ranks stocks by expected total return, not just yield. Valuation matters. If that's the part you value, FAST Graphs is worth a look.

It plots years of earnings, cash flow and dividends against the share price, so you can see at a glance whether a stock is cheap or expensive versus its own history, and whether earnings still cover the dividend. Its pricing page shows Basic at $15.95 a month and Premium at $40 a month, with a 7 day free trial and no card required. Dividend investors will usually want Premium, since the dividend research lives there.

Pick it if: you're a dividend growth investor who wants to do your own valuation work instead of reading someone else's conclusions.

4. Snowball Analytics: If What You Really Want Is a Tracker

A lot of people sign up for research services and end up only using the portfolio view. If that's you, stop paying research prices for a tracker.

Snowball has a free plan with 1 portfolio and 10 holdings. Paid plans are $79.99 a year for Starter (unlimited holdings), $149.99 for Investor and $249.99 for Expert, with monthly billing available. You get a dividend calendar, income forecasts, yield on cost, broker syncing on paid plans and a dividend rating for a quick safety read. It covers 30+ currencies and 70+ exchanges.

It won't give you analyst reports. But it will show you exactly what's coming in each month, which is what most income investors check every week. More options are in my best free dividend tracker roundup.

Pick it if: you want to see all your dividend income in one place for under $100 a year.

5. The Free Stack: Lists, Screeners and a 10 Minute Safety Check

Here's what I'd do before paying anyone:

  • Use Sure Dividend's own free lists for ideas. The Dividend Champions, Aristocrats and monthly payer lists are free and updated often.
  • Check the basics yourself. Payout ratio, free cash flow coverage, debt trend and how the dividend held up in 2008 and 2020. Sites like StockAnalysis and Yahoo Finance show most of it.
  • Use a free screener to filter by yield and payout ratio. My best dividend screeners guide covers the good free ones.
  • Bookmark a few solid dividend sites. I ranked them in best dividend websites.

Pick it if: you own 5 to 20 stocks and don't mind a little homework.

My take on ETFs: Sure Dividend and most of these tools are built around individual stocks. If you own funds like $SCHD, $VYM or covered call ETFs like $JEPI, a stock safety score doesn't help much. The fund already spreads the risk. What matters is total return and whether the fund is paying you from real income or eating its own NAV. FAQ: Sure Dividend Alternatives

What are the best Sure Dividend alternatives?

Simply Safe Dividends ($468 a year) for dividend safety scores, Seeking Alpha Premium (about $299 a year list price) for broad stock research with Dividend Grades, FAST Graphs for valuation charts, Snowball Analytics (free plan, paid from $79.99 a year) for tracking with a dividend rating, and free tools if you mostly own ETFs.

How much does Sure Dividend cost?

Sure Dividend's top service, the Sure Analysis Research Database, was $999 a year after a 7 day free trial on its checkout page as of November 15, 2026. It includes all of Sure Dividend's premium newsletters. Its free articles and dividend lists cost nothing.

Is Sure Dividend worth it?

It can be if you buy individual dividend stocks often and will actually read the reports. Its 13 person team covers 900+ income securities with quarterly 3 page reports and a daily spreadsheet. If you mostly own ETFs, a $999 stock research database is hard to justify.

Does Sure Dividend have a free trial or refund?

Yes. The Sure Analysis Research Database offers a 7 day free trial, a full refund within the first 60 days, and prorated refunds after that, according to its subscribe page.

Is there a free alternative to Sure Dividend?

Yes. Sure Dividend's own free lists are a good start, StockAnalysis and Yahoo Finance show payout ratios and dividend history, and Snowball Analytics has a free tracker plan. A simple payout ratio, cash flow and debt check covers the basics for free.
