Simply Safe Dividends vs Snowball Analytics 2026: Which Is Worth Paying For?

By Benjie • Published November 12, 2026 • TopDividendTools.com

Simply Safe Dividends is the better pick if you own individual dividend stocks and your biggest fear is a dividend cut: its analyst-driven Dividend Safety Scores are the reason to pay $468 a year. Snowball Analytics is the better pick if you mainly want to track your income across accounts and holdings: it has a free plan, and unlimited holdings start at $79.99 a year. They look like competitors, but they're really built for two different jobs.

I see this question come up all the time, usually phrased as "which dividend trackers offer better features or pricing than Snowball?" or "is Simply Safe Dividends worth it?" Both are good tools. I'm not here to trash either one. But one is a research service that happens to track your portfolio, and the other is a portfolio tracker that happens to have a dividend rating. Once you see that, the choice gets a lot easier.

Pricing: The $388 a Year Question

Let's start with money, because that's where these two are furthest apart.

Simply Safe Dividends has one plan: $39 a month, billed annually at $468. There's no monthly option, and the company says it doesn't run discounts or specials. You do get a 14 day free trial with no credit card, and first-time subscribers get a 60 day money-back guarantee, which is generous.

So Snowball's Starter plan costs about $388 a year less than Simply Safe Dividends. Even Snowball's top Expert tier is $218 a year cheaper. Over 10 years, that Starter gap is almost $3,900. That's real money for a dividend investor. At a 4% yield, it's roughly the income from another $9,700 invested.

This is the part most people miss, though. Price only matters relative to what the tool does for you. If Simply Safe Dividends helps you dodge one big dividend cut in a stock you own a lot of, it can pay for itself. If you own mostly ETFs and just want to see your income in one place, you're paying for research you won't use.

Dividend Safety: Where Simply Safe Dividends Earns Its Price

This is the whole reason Simply Safe Dividends exists. Its Dividend Safety Scores rate each company from 0 to 100, in five bands:

  • 81 to 100: Very Safe, very unlikely to be cut
  • 61 to 80: Safe, unlikely to be cut
  • 41 to 60: Borderline, moderate risk of a cut
  • 21 to 40: Unsafe, heightened risk
  • 0 to 20: Very Unsafe, high risk of a cut

The scores look at payout ratios, debt and coverage, recession performance, dividend longevity, industry cyclicality, free cash flow and forward analyst estimates. And they're not a black-box quant number. They're overseen by founder Brian Bollinger, a CPA and former equity analyst who started the service in 2015.

What I like most is that they publish their track record. According to Simply Safe Dividends, investors who stuck with companies scoring above 60 since 2015 would have avoided 97% of dividend cuts: 929 of 950. They also list recent cuts with the score each company had before the announcement. That kind of transparency is rare.

Snowball's answer is its dividend rating, which comes with every paid plan. It's useful as a quick gut check on a holding. But it's part of a tracker, not a research product. You don't get analyst write-ups, cut alerts with commentary, or a public track record behind it the way you do with Simply Safe Dividends.

Winner on dividend safety: Simply Safe Dividends, and it isn't close. If this is the feature you care about, Snowball doesn't really replace it.

Portfolio and Income Tracking: Where Snowball Pulls Ahead

Flip the question and the answer flips too.

Snowball is built around tracking. Every plan, including Free, gets automatic dividend tracking, a dividend calendar, received payouts, yield and yield on cost, custom withholding tax, IRR and performance including fees and dividends, diversification breakdowns, backtests and CSV export. It covers 30+ currencies and 70+ exchanges, so it works for people who own international stocks too. And on paid plans you can link your brokerage instead of importing reports by hand.

Simply Safe Dividends does track your portfolio too. You get unlimited portfolios, an income calendar and forecast, dividend change alerts and customizable columns. For a U.S. dividend stock investor, that covers the basics well. But its pages describe coverage of U.S. stocks, funds, bonds and CDs, and brokerage linking isn't one of the features listed on its pricing page. If you hold foreign stocks or want automatic syncing, Snowball is the safer bet.

Winner on tracking: Snowball Analytics, especially for multiple accounts, ETFs and international holdings.

Research and Stock Ideas

Simply Safe Dividends includes a lot of research for the price: analyst notes when a score changes, stock and closed-end fund screeners, valuation charts that compare today's yield to its 5 year average, 10 year financial charts, S&P credit ratings, and a quarterly newsletter with model portfolios. The whole philosophy is conservative. No yield chasing, no hype, just safe income.

Snowball gives you data, not opinions: 5 years of company fundamentals on Free and Starter, 10 years on Investor and Expert, a "top dividend stocks" list with filters, and backtests going back 10 years on lower plans or 30+ years on the higher ones.

If you want someone to do the thinking with you on individual stocks, that's Simply Safe Dividends. If you'd rather do your own analysis with good data, Snowball is enough. For a broader look at research tools, see my best dividend screeners roundup.

My take on ETFs: neither tool is built to answer the question that matters most for high yield ETFs: is the fund paying you out of real income, or slowly eating its own NAV? A safety score is for companies. For income ETFs, look at total return and price decay. That's what TopDividendETFsPRO is for, and you can check any fund's real return on ETFTotalReturns.com.

Who Each Tool Is Best For

Pick Simply Safe Dividends if:

  • You own a portfolio of individual U.S. dividend stocks, and a cut would really hurt your income.
  • You're retired or close to it and want conservative, analyst-backed guidance.
  • You'll actually use the alerts, screeners and newsletter, not just the tracker.
  • You're fine paying $468 once a year.

Pick Snowball Analytics if:

  • You mostly own ETFs, or a mix of ETFs and stocks, and want to see all your income in one place.
  • You have several brokerage accounts or international holdings.
  • You want to start free, or keep costs under $100 a year.
  • You want a phone app and monthly billing as an option.

Use both if:

You have a large individual stock portfolio. Some investors track everything in Snowball and use Simply Safe Dividends purely as the safety check before they buy or add. That's about $548 a year combined at the entry levels, so it only makes sense if your portfolio is big enough for one avoided cut to cover it.

FAQ: Simply Safe Dividends vs Snowball Analytics

Is Simply Safe Dividends better than Snowball Analytics?

For dividend cut protection on individual U.S. stocks, yes: its analyst-driven Dividend Safety Scores are the core of the service, and the company reports they avoided 97% of dividend cuts since 2015. For tracking income across accounts, ETFs and international holdings, Snowball Analytics is better and much cheaper.

How much does Simply Safe Dividends cost compared to Snowball Analytics?

As of November 2026, Simply Safe Dividends costs $468 a year ($39 a month billed annually) with no monthly option. Snowball Analytics has a free plan, then Starter at $79.99 a year, Investor at $149.99 and Expert at $249.99, or $7.99, $14.99 and $24.99 billed monthly.

Which dividend trackers offer better features or pricing than Snowball Analytics?

Few trackers beat Snowball on price for unlimited holdings at $79.99 a year. Simply Safe Dividends offers deeper dividend safety research but costs $468 a year. Sharesight is stronger on tax reports and performance math. See our Snowball Analytics alternatives guide for the full list.

Does Snowball Analytics have dividend safety scores?

Snowball has a dividend rating on its paid plans, starting with Starter at $79.99 a year. It is a quick read built into the tracker, not an analyst-driven research product like Simply Safe Dividends' Dividend Safety Scores.

Is there a free trial for Simply Safe Dividends and Snowball Analytics?

Yes. Both offer a 14 day free trial with no credit card required. Simply Safe Dividends also has a 60 day money-back guarantee for first-time subscribers, and Snowball Analytics has a permanent free plan with 1 portfolio and 10 holdings.
